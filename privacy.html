Twilyte

Privacy Policy

Last updated September 22, 2026

Twilyte doesn't track you. There's no account, no advertising, no analytics software, and no profile built about you or your device. This page explains, in full, everything the app does with data — because that list is short enough to just say plainly.

Location

Twilyte uses your device's location (with your permission, via your browser's or operating system's location prompt) to calculate the Sun, Moon, and star positions, twilight times, and light-pollution estimate for where you are. Your coordinates are used as follows:

  • On your device: all astronomy calculations run locally in the app — your position is never sent to a Twilyte server, because Twilyte doesn't operate one.
  • To Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), a weather-forecast provider: your coordinates, and a point about 150 km from them toward the sunset, are sent to fetch the cloud forecast used for the "Clear & Dark" observing score and the sunset colour, and to search for places by name. Open-Meteo does not require an account or API key and is not used for any purpose beyond serving that request.

To say when the International Space Station passes over, Twilyte also fetches the station's published orbit from CelesTrak (celestrak.org), at most every 12 hours. That request is the same for everyone: it contains no location or anything else about you, and the passes are worked out on your device. Like any web request it reaches CelesTrak from your IP address.

For the northern and southern lights it fetches the geomagnetic forecast published by the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's Space Weather Prediction Center (swpc.noaa.gov), at most every 3 hours. That request is also the same for everyone and contains nothing about you; whether the aurora could reach your sky is worked out on your device.

You can deny location access entirely and still use Twilyte by entering a ZIP code, searching for a place by name, or typing coordinates directly.

Motion sensors and camera

Two features in the Stars tab can use hardware beyond location. Both are optional, both ask before they start, and neither sends anything anywhere.

  • Compass and motion sensors power Aim Assist and Sky View, which work out which way your phone is pointed so Twilyte can show you where a star is. The readings are used as they arrive and are not stored or transmitted. The one thing that is saved is a manual compass correction, if you set one — a single number, kept on your device.
  • The camera is off by default. If you switch it on in Sky View, the live view from your rear camera is drawn behind the star overlay so you can line the two up. Nothing is photographed, recorded, saved, or uploaded — the image is displayed and discarded, frame by frame. Turning the camera off, or leaving Sky View, stops it.

What's stored on your device

Your preferences and your last-used location are saved locally in your browser's or app's storage, so Twilyte remembers them between visits. None of this is transmitted anywhere — it never leaves your device except as described above.

  • Your saved and current location
  • Display preferences (12/24-hour time, red-light night mode, sky-brightness setting)
  • Your Aim Assist compass correction, if you've set one
  • The space station's orbit, for up to a week, so passes can be shown offline
  • The geomagnetic forecast, for up to two days, for the aurora
  • Your observing log: what you've marked as seen, and when
  • A short-lived cache (about an hour) of the weather forecast, to avoid re-fetching it constantly
  • Whether you've dismissed onboarding messages
  • When this browser was last counted by the visit counter, and the last total it showed, so that coming back later the same day isn't counted as another visit

You can clear all of this at any time by clearing your browser's site data for twilyte.info, or by deleting the app.

Anonymous visit counter

Twilyte shows a running count of visits on the Console tab. Loading the page pings a small counting service at most once a day; reloading the page, or leaving and coming back later the same day, doesn't ping it again. This is on the website only: the iOS and Android apps don't show the count and never contact the counting service. The website sends it nothing beyond what any web request inherently includes — not your location, not a device identifier.

Like any web server, the counting service receives your IP address with that request. So that it doesn't count the same visitor twice in one day, it keeps a one-way, salted hash of the address — never the address itself — for 24 hours, after which it's deleted automatically. For an IPv6 address only the first half, the part that identifies your network rather than your device, goes into the hash. The hash isn't linked to your location or to anything else, and is used for nothing but the count.

In-app purchase (Pro features, iOS & Android app only)

The native iOS and Android apps offer an optional one-time purchase to unlock the multi-night observing planner. Purchases are processed entirely by Apple's App Store or Google Play — Twilyte never receives your payment details. To verify and restore your purchase across devices, the app uses RevenueCat, which is assigned an anonymous, random identifier for your installation (not your name, email, or Apple/Google account) along with the purchase receipt Apple or Google already issued. See RevenueCat's privacy policy for how they handle that data.

What Twilyte does not do

  • No account, sign-in, name, or email is ever collected
  • No advertising SDKs and no ad tracking
  • No analytics software (no Google Analytics, no crash reporters, no session recording)
  • No cookies — the app uses only local on-device storage described above
  • No data is sold, shared, or used for any purpose beyond making the app work

Children's privacy

Twilyte is a general-audience reference tool and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Since the app collects no personal information from any user, no age-specific handling is needed.

Changes to this policy

If what Twilyte collects or how it's used changes, this page will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change accordingly.
